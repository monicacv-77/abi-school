Units follow what Abi is studying in her in-person history class. Each unit mixes the four case types, ends with a Simulation where it fits, and closes with a Supervisor unit review of its big ideas.

**Big ideas** (what the unit review asks about, one question each):
1. North America was already populated by established societies with governments, trade, farming, technology and religion. The Powhatan were a political and economic power. (002, 005)
2. The Columbian Exchange permanently changed the world: plants, animals, people and diseases crossed the Atlantic both ways, helping some peoples and devastating others. (002, with the English livestock evidence and the end reveal; 005)
3. Survival depended on geography, resources and relationships. Outcomes rarely come from one decision, and solving one problem can create another. (001, 002, 004, 005)
4. Colonization fundamentally changed Native life: every settlement story has at least two sides. (002; 005, with "Meanwhile, at Werowocomoco" moments during play and "The Powhatan side" in the debrief)

Set aside for now, because the cases don't get into them: colonization driven by economic and political ambitions; colonial America's opportunity and exploitation (tobacco, 1619).

## Unit 2: Road to Independence (drafting)
Theme: why proud Englishmen ended up declaring independence. Builds Abi's skepticism of propaganda and persuasion, following on from Too Good to Be True. Cases are in date order.

**Big ideas** (draft, Oct 21; for Monica to review):
1. The fight was about who decides, not how much. Colonists had taxed and governed themselves through their own elected assemblies for over a century, so taxes passed by a Parliament they did not elect felt like a threat to their rights, even when the taxes were small. (006, 010)
2. Each move made the next one worse: taxes → protests and boycotts → crackdowns and soldiers → violence, until compromise seemed impossible. Big breaks come from a chain of actions and reactions, not one event. (006, 007, 008, 010)
3. Persuasion shaped the Revolution. Pictures, pamphlets, newspapers and rumors turned events into stories that moved people, and each side told the same event very differently. (007, 009)
4. Ordinary people made resistance work through networks: boycotts, committees, printers, riders and town meetings. (006, 008, 009)
5. Independence was a dangerous choice that divided people, and its promise of liberty and equality left many out: Loyalists, enslaved people, women and Native nations. (009, 010)

**Cases:**
1. 🔎 006 No Taxation Without… Wait, What? (Feb 1774), **drafted, PILOT** (Test play from the Parent dashboard; hidden from Abi until READY). Abi is Lord North's investigator in Boston after the Tea Party. 12 evidence items, real witnesses (Governor Hutchinson, printer Benjamin Edes) and invented ones. Guardrails: 5 evidence, 4 reasons, one explained as how it works. Twist: Lord North's letter says the dumped tea was cheaper than ever. Finale: advise Lord North what to do about Boston, then learn what he actually did (closed the port).
2. 🔎 007 The Boston Massacre (1770): Revere's engraving vs. the witness accounts; John Adams defended the soldiers.
3. 🛠️ 008 One If by Land (1775): design an alarm network to warn the towns around Boston (riders, lanterns, bells, routes, timing).
4. 🛠️ 009 Go Viral, 1776 (January 1776): Abi is a Philadelphia printer spreading Thomas Paine's *Common Sense*. She chooses the message style, price, print run and distribution. Stress tests: a reader who can't read, a Loyalist reply, the British going after printers, a paper shortage, a reader who thinks it goes too far.
5. 🧭 010 Philadelphia, 1776 (July 1776, finale): Abi is a delegate to the Second Continental Congress; signing means treason.
- 💡 Inquiry open throughout; the Wonder List carries over.

## Later units (ideas)
- **The Constitution:** 🛠️ Build a Government (1787). Abi designs a government after the Articles of Confederation fail. Stress tests: a would-be king, big vs. small states, a tax revolt (Shays' Rebellion), a war, a law that violates rights. Teaches checks and balances.
- **Westward / the young nation:** 🛠️ Pack for the Unknown (Lewis and Clark, 1803–06). Abi outfits the Corps of Discovery: weight limits, budget, a needs checklist.

## Process for each unit and case
Write the unit's big ideas → draft each case to the Case Design Standard → run the case checker → run the simulated students → Monica test-plays → READY.
